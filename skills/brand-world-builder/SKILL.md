# Brand World Builder

## Source
Varun Mayya, *The Most Valuable Skill After AI* — identified as **Secret Kata #2** from the talk itself (YouTube, September 2026): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4N8WadMsdmI
This is a Secret Kata, not a numbered Kata. A distilled, paraphrased method — not a substitute for watching the talk.

## Overview
A brand is a story that enough people believe. When products are easy to build, the world around them is the moat. Strong worlds have a contradictory premise, clear roles, a hierarchy, a barrier to entry, their own language and rituals, consequences for breaking character, consistent laws, some chance, and room for interpretation.

## When to use
- **Brand / product:** positioning in a copyable category; a product universe or launch lore.
- **Community:** designing entry, ranks, rituals, and in-group language.
- **Culture:** shaping team behaviour through language, rituals, standards, and hierarchy.
- **Creators / personal brand:** a consistent world audiences return to.

## When not to use
- One persuasive piece of writing to a specific reader (email, pitch, post) → use **reader-world-builder**.
- An instruction or brief for an executor → use **literal-genie-brief**.
- Building reach and touchpoints for an existing brand → use **lightning-catcher-distribution**.
- A team that is fearful or political → fix safety first with **social-safety-regulator**.

## Steps
1. **Premise (the "yes, but").** Write the contradiction at the heart of the brand: "It's ___, yet it ___." No tension = flat story.
2. **Fill the world canvas.** For each, write 1–3 lines (mark "missing" if absent):
   - **Characters/archetypes** people can identify with.
   - **Hierarchy** — how members move up or down.
   - **Entry & access** — what it takes to get in (money, skill, initiation, referral).
   - **Language** — unique terms insiders use.
   - **Rituals** — recurring moments (launches, ceremonies, unboxings, demo days).
   - **Shaming/standards** — what's embarrassing inside this world (sets behaviour).
   - **Mechanics/laws** — the core loop of how value is created and flows.
   - **Chance** — rarity, drops, surprises.
   - **Everyday** — what members do routinely between big events.
   - **Lore** — fixed facts / flexible interpretations / legends.
   - **Technology** — tools that define the medium.
   - **Eras** — milestones and nostalgia.
   - **Aesthetic** — a style kept from day one, regardless of budget.
   - **Exit penalty** — what members lose if they leave.
3. **Consistency check.** List any rule the brand breaks. Believable beats realistic: audiences accept the unreal if it's consistent.
4. **Interpretation vs exposition.** Decide what to explain and what to leave open for the audience to fill (community theories, fan creations).
5. **Story machine.** Design rules, resources, and competing factions/needs that generate stories without you scripting each one (community programs, creator ecosystems, autonomous internal teams).
6. **Plan for forks and retcons.** Decide what is canon and how you'll handle splits (spin-offs, sub-communities). When telling your origin story, know you're retconning; keep it honest.
7. **Culture application.** Use the same canvas on a team: what language, rituals, standards, and hierarchy shape behaviour? Constraints produce the behaviours you want.

## Output
Premise line, filled canvas with gaps flagged, the three highest-leverage additions (e.g., a ritual, a term, an entry criterion), and consistency risks.

## Caution
Shaming and exit penalties can turn toxic; use standards that motivate without humiliating (see **social-safety-regulator**).
