# Belief to Behavior

## Source
Varun Mayya, *Why Belief Matters More Than Power & Money* (YouTube, November 2026): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jreQIik5jlE
Labelled here as **Kata 14 (inferred)**: the talk sits in the official Katas playlist after Kata 13 and refers to itself as a Kata, but its title carries no number. A distilled, paraphrased method — not a substitute for watching the talk.

## Overview
Belief matters, but it shows up only as repeated action. You become what you repeatedly do. Hold ambitious, harmless beliefs; convert them into behaviour; protect them from premature social rejection; let real evidence — not opinions — decide; and stay willing to update.

## When to use
- **Career / founders:** ambition without action; conviction shaken by rejections (investors, recruiters, clients).
- **Creators:** early dips in views or traction tempting a quit.
- **Goal-setting:** turning a big goal into stacked, fundable rungs.
- **Hubris check:** the next escalation after early wins.

## When not to use
- The person is still choosing *between* options → use **decide-and-commit**.
- The person asks whether a specific move is too risky or how to stage it → use **risk-staging-playbook**.
- The person needs evidence of their own credibility level or a clean next attempt → **scouter-competence-read** complements this.
- Signs of depression or crisis → this is not mental-health support; encourage professional help (see **social-safety-regulator** for the leadership lens).

## Steps
1. **Classify the belief.** *Type one:* ambitious, harms no one, slightly out of reach but mathematically achievable → pursue. *Type two:* harms others or yourself → drop. *Hubris check:* is this the next escalation after wins, into a business with a dangerous cash-flow or risk profile? If so, study precedents first.
2. **Convert to behaviour.** What will you *do* daily/weekly that a person who already achieved this would do? Replace "if I had X resources I would…" with what you can do with current resources.
3. **Build a stacked plan.** Break the goal into rungs where each rung funds or enables the next (A → B → C). Start the first rung now. Keep the full ambition private if sharing it would invite dismissal.
4. **Win small to grow belief.** Pick goals slightly beyond current ability; each completed one is evidence. Find people in your circle who've done similar things — beliefs are social.
5. **Protect belief.** Expect early dips (e.g., content views dropping after week one). Don't let a batch of rejections decide; they're opinions. Keep the team insulated from your doubts while staying honest about facts.
6. **Evidence ledger.** Two columns: "People say" vs. "Reality shows" (usage, revenue, results). Act on the second column. Consensus can be wrong; reality is the judge.
7. **Commit for compounding.** Compare options on long-run compounding, not this month's cash (e.g., a stake in a proven fast-growing thing vs. scattered gigs). Reduce optionality that dilutes commitment.
8. **Hold loosely.** Write: "What new evidence would make me change course?" Review periodically. Changing your mind on new evidence is rational.

## Output
Belief classification, behaviour plan, stacked rungs, protection plan, evidence-ledger template, and update triggers.

## Questions to ask
- "What would someone who already did this be doing this week?"
- "Is the 'no' you heard evidence, or an opinion?"
- "If you're wrong, how will you find out?"
