# Lightning Catcher Distribution

## Source
Varun Mayya, *How Distribution Can 10x Your Luck, Career & Income | KATA 10* (YouTube, July 2026): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGKgCLVxRCk
A distilled, paraphrased method — not a substitute for watching the talk.

**On the numbers:** Varun attributed the touchpoint math to a Google "7-11-4"-style rule of thumb (roughly 7 hours of content, 11 touchpoints, 4 platforms) and said he was unsure of the exact figures. Treat every touch count and engagement benchmark below as **directional**, not as measured law.

## Overview
Distribution (an audience that trusts you) multiplies the value of whatever you sell — products, services, or yourself. Luck can be engineered by increasing surface area. Trust accumulates over many touchpoints and can't be rushed or precisely measured.

## When to use
- **Career:** wanting recruiters, collaborators, or opportunities to come inbound.
- **Freelance / services:** building a client pipeline that isn't cold outreach.
- **Venture / product:** deciding to build an owned audience before or alongside a product.
- **Marketing:** content that "isn't working yet"; planning platforms, cadence, and what to measure.
- **Org buy-in:** making the long-term case for brand/content to short-tenure decision-makers.

## When not to use
- The question is *what caused* a past result or whether to cut a channel → use **true-attribution-check**. Tie-break: lightning-catcher = build future distribution; attribution = diagnose past results honestly. If someone wants to kill content because "it shows no ROI," run attribution first, then this.
- The question is what to build or which venture bet to place → use **bootstrap-bet-engine**.
- The audience exists and the issue is the persuasiveness of one piece → use **reader-world-builder**.

## Steps
1. **What should the audience eventually do?** Buy, hire, partner, refer, invest, donate. Note ticket size and consideration level.
2. **Estimate the trust budget (directional).**
   - Cheap impulse product: ~5–8 touches.
   - Mid-ticket consumer: ~11 touches across ~4 channels.
   - High-consideration consumer (education, insurance, property): 12–27 touches, 20+ hours of content.
   - B2B vendor shortlist: ~17 interactions; large B2B deals: 60–100+.
   - Hiring decisions work the same way: "I keep seeing this person around."
   Conclusion: one post won't do it; plan for repetition across platforms.
3. **Define the value units.** Each piece is either **utility** (teaches/solves) or **entertainment**. Pick a mix and a consistent theme so repeated exposure compounds into recognition.
4. **Design surface area.** Which 2–4 platforms does the target audience use? What cadence is sustainable for 12–24 months? What deeper layers exist for high-trust fans (long-form, behind the scenes, community)?
5. **Measure with proxies, not ROI.** Engagement (likes+comments+shares) as % of views: ~10% good, 20%+ excellent (directional). Watch time / retention past the first seconds. Share rate (strongest intent signal). Reach to non-followers; reach as % of followers. Qualitative: inbound messages, "I saw your…" at events, deal flow.
6. **Turn deal flow into opportunities.** Log inbound requests. When several people ask for the same thing, that's a product/service signal.
7. **Guard against the usual failures.** Expecting immediate ROI above impulse price; going transactional (only selling) and eroding trust; quitting before trust accumulates; org incentive mismatch (short-tenure decision-makers prefer measurable ads — make the long-term case explicitly).

## Output
Goal, trust budget, platform + cadence plan, content value mix, proxy metric targets, 90-day and 12-month checkpoints.

## Questions to ask
- "If an opportunity appeared in your field tomorrow, would it find you?"
- "How many hours of your thinking has your ideal client seen?"

## Worked example
*An independent UX researcher wants three new B2B clients a quarter without cold email.*
- Goal: get hired for a high-consideration B2B service → trust budget is large (tens of touches; treat as directional).
- Value units: 70% utility (teardown of one onboarding flow per week, with a concrete fix), 30% entertainment (short "spot the UX bug" posts).
- Surface area: LinkedIn (weekly teardown + 2 short posts), a monthly long-form newsletter for deeper trust, one community talk per quarter. Cadence chosen to be sustainable for 18 months.
- Proxies: share rate on teardowns, newsletter replies, inbound DMs mentioning a specific post. No ROI judgement before ~6 months.
- Checkpoints: 90 days — 12 teardowns published, first unsolicited inbound; 12 months — inbound covers most new clients. Repeated requests for "audit our signup flow" become a fixed-price productised offer.
