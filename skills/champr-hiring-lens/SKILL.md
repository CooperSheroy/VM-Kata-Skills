# CHAMPR Hiring Lens

## Source
Varun Mayya, *How to Get Hired Faster Than Everyone Else | Kata 8* (YouTube, April 2026): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oQd7Dw47VdU
A distilled, paraphrased method — not a substitute for watching the talk.

## Overview
People aren't interchangeable once trained. Hire for attitude, learning agility, and judgment; skills come last. Proof (portfolio, outcomes, referrals) beats claims (CVs). Entry level = potential; senior = proven expertise.

## When to use
- **Hiring:** scoring a specific candidate; deciding hire / no / test further.
- **Process design:** structured interviews, live tasks, referral use, whether a role should exist.
- **Retention:** sorting A/B/C players and designing rewards for As.
- **Job-seekers:** fixing a portfolio, calibrating the ask, making the recruiter's job easy.

## When not to use
- You want a general read on someone's credibility outside a hiring decision (co-founder, boss, mentor, yourself) → use **scouter-competence-read**. Tie-break: CHAMPR = *hire/keep for this role?*; scouter = *what level has this person earned through attempts?* In a hire, scouter evidence feeds the P (Potential) and R (Reassurance) scores here.
- The problem is team friction after hiring → use **coordination-cost-audit**.
- The problem is people fearing feedback or reviews → use **social-safety-regulator**.

## Steps
**Mode A — Evaluate a candidate (CHAMPR scorecard, 1–5 each, with evidence)**
- **C Cultural fit** — will they work the way the team works?
- **H Hard skills** — shown in portfolio or a live task, not the CV.
- **A Attitude** — do they actually want to do *this* work?
- **M Motivation/agency** — daily energy; do they act without being pushed?
- **P Potential** — evidence they grow; proven or only claimed?
- **R Reassurance** — is their read of situations accurate? Would you trust "it'll be fine" from them?
Also check: learning agility, ability to understand and implement, customer-first, diligence, taste.

*Live tests:*
- Give a realistic task and watch them use an AI tool: their prompts reveal understanding, desire, collaboration, speed of learning, and taste.
- "Walk me through an outcome you created, in detail — what you did, what changed, numbers."
- "Here's a problem; what would you decide?" Prefer people who recommend, not just ask.

**Mode B — Design the process**
1. Is the role certain to exist in a year? If not, don't hire for it.
2. Structured interview: same questions/tasks for all candidates.
3. Make it rigorous; passing should feel earned.
4. Tell the company's real story so expectations are set.
5. Don't rush: early hires for a new role may miss; keep the bar.
6. Use referrals; referrers stake reputation.

**Mode C — Retention (A/B/C test)**
Imagine each person resigning: panic = A, "takes months to replace" = B, relief = C. Goal: no Cs. For As: above-market base, uncapped incentives, offered before they ask. Watch for victim, messiah, and ostrich syndromes, and for burnout from doing work they don't enjoy.

**Mode D — Coach a job-seeker**
1. Is the target role portfolio-based or intangible (sales/management)? Portfolio-based → build 3–5 strong public pieces; this beats college and CV. Intangible → build referrals and brand-name proof; quantify outcomes.
2. Calibrate the ask: entry roles pay for potential; senior pay requires proof. Don't oversell a weak portfolio.
3. Make the recruiter's job easy: clear fit, links, specific outcomes.
4. Break the "safety blanket": ship something real, even if imperfect.

## Output
CHAMPR table with evidence, top risk, decision (hire / no / test further), and one question to resolve the biggest uncertainty. (Modes B–D: the process outline, A/B/C list with actions, or a job-seeker action plan.)

## Red flags
- Evaluating only from the CV.
- Hiring for a role that may not exist in a year.
- Different questions for different candidates.
- Keeping a C because replacing them is uncomfortable.

## Worked example
*A 12-person e-commerce company is hiring its first growth marketer; finalist has a strong CV from a big brand.*

| | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| C | 3 | Used to large-team approvals; asked twice who signs off on spend |
| H | 4 | Live task: built a sensible 2-week test plan for one product line |
| A | 4 | Asked to see last month's actual numbers before suggesting anything |
| M | 2 | Every past win described as "we"; no self-started projects |
| P | 3 | Growth claimed, not shown — same scope for four years |
| R | 3 | Called a 3x ROAS target "easy" without seeing margins |

Top risk: agency (M) — a small team needs someone who moves without a process. Decision: **test further** — a paid one-week trial project with a real budget cap. Question to resolve: "Tell me about something you started that nobody asked you to."
