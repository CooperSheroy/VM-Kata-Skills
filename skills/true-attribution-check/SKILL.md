# True Attribution Check

## Source
Varun Mayya, *Why Indian Startups Are Unprofitable | KATA 6* (YouTube, March 2026): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEnxvZXzo0E
A distilled, paraphrased method — not a substitute for watching the talk. The India startup framing is the talk's example; the method is general.

## Overview
The easiest thing to measure is rarely the thing that caused the result. Correct attribution makes success repeatable; wrong attribution makes you double down on the wrong lever.

## When to use
- **Marketing / growth:** budget allocation, ROAS or last-click dashboards, "the ads are working."
- **Post-mortems:** explaining a launch, campaign, or quarter that succeeded or failed.
- **Team:** credit for a win; performance reviews built on one visible metric.
- **Personal:** whether a degree, course, routine, or relationship is really the lever for the outcome you want.

## When not to use
- The question is how to *build* audience and trust going forward (platforms, cadence, touchpoints) → use **lightning-catcher-distribution**. Tie-break: attribution = *what actually caused this result, and where should spend move?* (backward-looking diagnosis); lightning-catcher = *how do I build distribution that produces future results?* Use attribution first if someone wants to cut brand/content spend because it's hard to measure.
- The diagnosis is about a vague brief producing wrong work → use **literal-genie-brief**.

## Steps
1. **State the outcome and the claimed cause.** "X happened because of Y." Write both explicitly.
2. **Interrogate the evidence source.** Is Y favoured because it's measurable (dashboards, last click, coupon codes, form answers)? Who benefits from this explanation (a manager's KPI, a vendor, ego)? Was the data collected from distracted people tapping a form?
3. **Map the full journey.** List every touchpoint and contributor before the outcome: content seen months earlier, reviews, comparisons, word of mouth, team members. Assume multi-touch unless proven otherwise.
4. **Counterfactual tests.** If Y were removed, would the outcome change — has that been tested? Do others doing Y get the outcome (if hard work alone caused wealth, all labourers would be rich)? Could a coincident factor explain it (timing, market shift, growing up)?
5. **Pick an intent proxy.** Choose the metric closest to intent for the channel (shares/forwards for social content, qualified inbound, repeat purchase) rather than views or likes. In-person observation is strong evidence.
6. **Qualitative attribution.** Talk to real buyers and non-buyers: "What made you choose / not choose us? What else did you look at?" Have an honest, unbiased person synthesise patterns.
7. **Watch for traps.**
   - *Performance-ad spiral:* rising acquisition costs, shrinking margins, price hikes → no moat.
   - *Measurability bias:* cutting brand/story spend because it's hard to measure.
   - *Abandoning the real engine* after success (e.g., dropping organic content once "professional").
   - *Advertising a weak product* — it sends buyers to compare and pick a competitor.
   - *Credit grabbing* in teams.
8. **Recommend reallocation.** State what to do more of, less of, and what experiment would confirm the attribution.

**Personal-life version:** apply the same steps to degrees, courses, skills, routines, or "my problem is person X" — does this lever actually correlate with the outcome you want?

**Default prior for business success:** untapped market + good talent executing well + surviving long enough. Be skeptical of any single "trick."

## Output
Claimed cause vs. likely causes, journey map, counterfactual verdict, chosen intent proxy, traps found, and a more / less / test reallocation.
