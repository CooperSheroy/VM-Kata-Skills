# Risk Staging Playbook

## Source
Varun Mayya, *How To Take Risk Like Rich People Do (1 Hour Masterclass) | Kata 9* (YouTube, April 2026): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWFn-RgxZ5M
A distilled, paraphrased method — not a substitute for watching the talk. Not financial advice; allocation ratios are directional.

## Overview
Don't take big risks — reduce risk until winning is likely, and pay to learn cheaply. Think in probabilities. Keep most of your position safe, avoid irreversible and "medium" exposures, and play where few others compete.

## When to use
- **Career:** quitting, switching fields, relocating, going freelance.
- **Venture:** launching, expanding, or taking a concentration risk (one big client or partner).
- **Money:** sizing a speculative bet against a safe core.
- **Business resilience:** "what if our biggest client leaves?" planning in good times.

## When not to use
- The person hasn't picked among several options yet → use **decide-and-commit** first.
- The person has picked a goal but lacks conviction or keeps stalling → use **belief-to-behavior**.
- The question is how to build a venture with no funding (portfolio of bets, distribution) → use **bootstrap-bet-engine**.
Tie-break: risk-staging = *one specific move: how risky, and how to stage it?*

## Steps
1. **Reframe as probabilities.** Write best / base / worst case **two years out** (not today's pay). Include life factors (health, family, cash needs). Estimate base rates through research (how many similar ventures survive? what happens to people in this role?).
2. **Check biases.** Overconfidence (would an outsider rate your odds lower?); tribal/crowd risk (comfortable only because peers do it, or scared only because they don't?); short-sightedness (3 years, not 3 weeks); recency/second-level (is everyone fearing the obvious thing while ignoring the real one?).
3. **Design the cheap experiment (GTO).** The cheapest — ideally free — test that reveals whether this works, with high payoff if it does. Define signal, budget, deadline. Scale investment only as probability rises; size each bet to its estimated probability.
4. **Protect the core (barbell).** Keep ~80–90% ultra-safe, ~10–20% in high-upside bets. Avoid medium risk: partial exposure with no shock absorber. Ensure a failure can't kill the parent (job, company, savings). Shock absorbers: months of runway, low burn, spare leads/pipeline.
5. **Irreversibility audit.** Which parts can't be undone? Slow those down. Are bridges being burned (badmouthing, leaving badly)? Don't. Counterparty risk: will this client/employer/partner exist in 6–12 months?
6. **Real vs fake optionality.** Fake: gathering offers you won't act on. Real: transferable skills (accurate risk-taking, hiring good people, sharing upside, selling) that let you adapt to shocks.
7. **Choose the table.** How many competitors? Fewer players → a decent hand wins and you gain time to build a lead. Random domain (apps, viral content, funding) → many small parallel shots; skill-based → invest in mastery. Unfamiliar field → budget ~2 years of learning.
8. **Ongoing paranoia.** In good times ask "what if our biggest client/income source leaves?" and prepare alternatives now.

## Output
Two-year scenario table, the cheap experiment, allocation (safe vs risky), irreversibility flags, competition read, and a go / stage / no-go recommendation.

## Red flags
- All-in on one irreversible move with no runway.
- Comparing only today's salary, not two-year outcomes.
- Burning bridges on the way out.
- Feeling safe because everyone around you is doing the same thing.
