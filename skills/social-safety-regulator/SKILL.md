# Social Safety Regulator

## Source
Varun Mayya, *Secret Kata #1 Harsh Truths About India's Social Pressure and Depression* (YouTube, January 2026): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM7jyLJBi_g
A distilled, paraphrased method — not a substitute for watching the talk.

**Guardrail:** this is a business and leadership lens, not medical advice. Do not diagnose or speculate about anyone's mental health; if someone shows signs of depression or crisis, encourage professional support. The talk's India-specific commentary is an example of a high rank-pressure culture, not a universal claim about any group.

## Overview
People under chronic rank pressure shift into threat mode: feedback feels like humiliation, silence like rejection, others' wins like losses. Many "attitude" problems are safety problems. A leader's job includes regulating the team's sense of social safety so people can collaborate and take calculated risks.

## When to use
- **Team:** fearful, political, or blame-heavy teams; people hiding mistakes.
- **Management:** delivering critical feedback, a rejection, or a denied promotion; designing review and promotion practices.
- **Hiring/retention:** high performers leaving because of culture, or stack-ranking fallout.
- **Self:** a founder or individual spiralling after rejection or failure and needing practical coping steps (not therapy).

## When not to use
- The issue is structural friction, unclear roles, or incentives rather than fear → use **coordination-cost-audit**.
- The person has lost conviction in a goal and needs a plan to act → use **belief-to-behavior**.
- Anything that looks like a clinical mental-health issue → recommend a qualified professional; this skill is not a substitute.

## Steps
1. **Spot threat-mode signals.** Overreaction to neutral messages or feedback; silence read as rejection; resentment of peers' wins and zero-sum talk; fear of trying, hiding mistakes, blame; hostile fight-everyone behaviour from people trying to climb.
2. **Look for the underlying current.** What need sits behind the surface conflict (status, fairness, recognition, certainty, being heard)? Address that, not just the topic.
3. **Audit the environment (score each 0–2).** Praise public, critique private? Critique targets work, not identity? Decisions explained? Evaluation and promotion criteria written and known (no "secret evaluation")? Post-mortems blameless? Wins shared and zero-sum players removed? Stack ranking or fear used as motivators? (Red flag.)
4. **Rewrite the interaction.** For a pending piece of feedback or a "no": acknowledge the reasoning, state the gap specifically, explain why, offer the path forward, deliver privately. For a rejection (funding, appraisal), separate "this decision" from "your worth."
5. **Personal tools under pressure.**
   - *Simulated threat:* write the worst case in detail and what you'd do; rehearsal dulls the fear.
   - *Distanced self-talk:* speak to yourself in the third person ("Name, here's what you'd tell a friend…") to move from threat to challenge.
   - A bad mood produces bad thoughts — fix sleep and state before trusting a spiral of conclusions.
6. **Design positive-sum structures.** Shared goals and rewards, clear ladders, collaboration incentives, and removing people who play zero-sum games.

## Output
Diagnosis (threat signals + underlying need), environment scorecard, the rewritten message or policy, and personal coping steps if relevant.

## Questions to ask
- "What need is this person protecting right now?"
- "Do people know exactly how they're evaluated?"
- "When something fails here, who gets blamed — and in front of whom?"
