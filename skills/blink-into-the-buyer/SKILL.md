# Blink Into the Buyer

## Source
Varun Mayya, *How to Think Like Rich People (1 Hour Masterclass) | KATA 13* (YouTube, August 2026): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WgipmPSPKk
A distilled, paraphrased method — not a substitute for watching the talk. Wealth segments and thresholds are rough, directional heuristics (the talk's figures are partly region-specific); verify against the actual person. Not financial advice.

## Overview
People with more capital operate under different constraints. They buy time, speed, attention, and freedom; they think in ownership and asymmetric bets; and they act on trust and referrals. Model their world ("blink"), not yours.

## When to use
- **Sales / partnerships:** premium offers, enterprise or HNI clients, approaching a senior decision-maker.
- **Networking:** getting in the door with powerful people; finding intro paths.
- **Personal money:** redefining "expensive"; moving from renting your time to owning assets.
- **Market choice:** checking whether a target audience can actually pay.

## When not to use
- The task is writing the actual pitch or email once you know what they value → follow up with **reader-world-builder**.
- The decision is whether a specific investment is too risky → use **risk-staging-playbook**.

## Steps
**Part A — Blink before you pitch**
1. Who is the person? Estimate their capital zone and dominant motive:
   - Security & status → safety, recognised status signals.
   - Freedom & recognition → comfort, convenience, being seen as successful.
   - Influence & access → skipping queues, industry impact, generational plans.
   - Legacy & trust → shaping industries, reputation, people they trust.
2. What do they already get flooded with? (Generic asks get ignored.)
3. What annoyance, time drain, or attention cost could you remove?
4. What would they need to believe to say yes — and can you pre-digest that (market size, proof, a trusted referrer)?
5. Rewrite the ask as: "This gives you back ___ (time / speed / attention / upside / legacy)."

**Part B — Getting in the door**
- Bring something useful first; give more than you receive early (a small free deliverable, an insight, an intro).
- Use credible intermediaries; referrals beat cold content.
- Be reliable and fair — trust compounds into deal flow and budgets.
- Get into the network; usefulness to people near the decision-maker travels upward.
- Build or join a team ("pack"); solo operators compete against systems.

**Part C — Personal money decisions**
- Redefine "expensive": does it cost attention/time/energy, or remove a recurring problem? Pay to remove annoyances that drain bandwidth when affordable.
- Shift from renting to owning: what assets (equity, content, IP, stocks) earn while you're not working?
- Prefer bets with capped downside and large upside; diversify so no single loss ends the game.
- Treat mistakes as tuition; stay curious about new technology early.

**Part D — Choosing markets**
Go where capital is concentrated relative to your offer. Check whether the target audience can actually pay.

## Output
A one-paragraph "blink" of the target, the reframed value proposition, a give-first move, an intro path, and (if personal) an ownership / asymmetric-bet plan.

## Caution
Segments are rough heuristics; verify with the actual person's signals. Don't fake status or manipulate.
