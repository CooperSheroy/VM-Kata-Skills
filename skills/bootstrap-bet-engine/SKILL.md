# Bootstrap Bet Engine

## Source
Varun Mayya, *Guide to Scaling A Startup with Zero Funding | Kata 1* (YouTube, December 2025): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9NjA41wShTI
A distilled, paraphrased method — not a substitute for watching the talk.

## Overview
Turn a vague "I want to build X" into a plan funded by customers: find a real behavioural shift, secure distribution, run several cheap bets, and keep the error margin survivable.

## When to use
- **Venture:** a founder or would-be founder with no (or little) capital deciding what to build or how to grow.
- **Funding:** "Should I raise?" — especially when the real question is whether anyone will pay.
- **Product portfolio:** choosing among several ideas or side projects; deciding which to kill.
- **Market check:** testing whether a category is saturated before entering it.
- **Next big bet:** an established operator choosing where to place a larger, multi-year bet.

## When not to use
- The question is purely "is this personal move too risky?" (quitting a job, a big investment) → use **risk-staging-playbook**.
- The project is already chosen and the problem is how to run it under uncertainty (team shape, R&D vs sure-shot work) → use **resolution-project-manager**.
- The plan is fine but bloated with features or initiatives → use **simplicity-filter**.
- The bottleneck is only audience/reach, not what to build → use **lightning-catcher-distribution**.

## Steps
1. **Find the shift (evidence, not vibes).**
   - What do ordinary people now spend *hours* on that they didn't 3–5 years ago? Where did attention move?
   - **Saturation test:** "What new app/product in this category have you (or 5 people you know) adopted in the last year?" If no one can answer, the category is saturated — a new product needs a structural shift to win; a distribution play may still be open.
   - Is the move "uncool" to peers? Low status often means low competition. Flag status-driven choices ("everyone in my scene is doing this").
2. **Distribution first, product still good.**
   - Who already has the audience you need? Can you *become* that audience owner (content, community, channels) before or while building product?
   - Require a concrete answer to "How will the first 1,000 people hear about this?" If missing, that is the first project.
3. **Portfolio of small bets.** Design 2–5 parallel experiments that each cost little enough that failure doesn't threaten the core, show a clear success signal in weeks (not years), and have a named owner. Rule: one thing at a time *per bet*, multiple bets at once. Kill quickly and without self-flagellation.
4. **Prove instead of persuade.** If investors/peers "don't get it," don't spend energy convincing. Find the cheapest revenue-backed proof. Ask: "If you proved it, would you still need the money?" Raise only for things revenue genuinely can't fund.
5. **Survive with near-zero error margin.** Keep costs low, don't over-hire, assume nobody is coming to save you. Separate what customers *say* they'll pay for from what they *do* pay for; trust payments and behaviour.
6. **Codify and compound.** When something works, write the playbook so person #2 and #3 can run it. Use the distribution base as a testing ground for the next idea.
7. **Choose the next big bet with a converging-trends check.** List independent trends pointing toward the bet. One trend = speculation; three or more pushing the same way = worth a serious bet.

## Output
1. The shift (with evidence) and saturation verdict.
2. Distribution plan for the first 1,000.
3. Bet portfolio table: bet / cost / signal / deadline / owner / kill rule.
4. Funding decision: prove-first vs raise, with reason.
5. Top risk (a competitor or automation that could wipe this out) and the signal to watch.

## Red flags
- Product-only plan with no distribution answer.
- One giant bet that would be fatal if wrong.
- Raising money to avoid finding out whether anyone will pay.
- Choosing location, peer group, or idea for status.

## Worked example
*A two-person team wants to build a habit-tracking app and raise a seed round.*
- Saturation test: none of the five friends asked has adopted a new habit app in a year → saturated as a pure product play.
- Shift found: their target users now spend hours in short fitness videos. The team already posts workout clips with ~8k followers → a distribution edge.
- Bets (each ≤ 3 weeks, ≤ a small fixed budget): (a) paid 30-day challenge sold to followers — signal: 50 paid sign-ups, kill if < 15; (b) printable tracker sold as a one-off digital product — signal: 100 sales; (c) weekly live session — signal: 20% repeat attendance.
- Funding: prove first — if (a) works, revenue covers the app build; raise only if the build cost clearly exceeds what revenue can fund.
- Top risk: a large fitness platform adds the same challenge format; watch for copycat challenges in the niche.
