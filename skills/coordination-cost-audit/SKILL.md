# Coordination Cost Audit

## Source
Varun Mayya, *Why 90% of Companies Break After 50 People | Kata 2* (YouTube, December 2025): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uojkYJz7d-k
A distilled, paraphrased method — not a substitute for watching the talk.

## Overview
Output depends less on individual talent than on whether everyone's **coordination cost** sits below the threshold the work needs. The leader's job: generate energy ("current"), remove blockages, lower coordination cost, and build a shared closed system.

## When to use
- **Team / scale:** a growing team where quality, speed, or morale is dropping.
- **Leadership:** a manager asking how to energise or align people, or why a star hire is making things worse.
- **Incentives:** designing pay, status, or mastery incentives for key people.
- **Agency / services:** adding clients or projects while earlier ones degrade.
- **Culture:** checking whether the team has shared direction, rituals, enforced rules, and outside validation.

## When not to use
- The problem is fear, politics, or people taking feedback as humiliation → use **social-safety-regulator** (they combine well).
- The problem is too many products, tools, or processes → use **simplicity-filter**.
- The work keeps coming back wrong because briefs are vague → use **literal-genie-brief**.
- The question is who to hire or whom to let go → use **champr-hiring-lens**.

## Steps
1. **Diagnose breadth vs depth.** List active projects/clients. Are new ones being added while earlier ones degrade? If yes, recommend narrowing to deep care of the most important ones before adding more.
2. **Person-by-person coordination-cost table.** For each key person: role / skill level / coordination-cost driver / fix.
   - Drivers: high ego or non-collaborative; low skill or wrong role; communication barrier; works on small issues before big ones; victim mindset; misaligned direction.
   - Fix options in order: clarify in writing → re-role toward mastery → upskill → incentive change → replace. Low skill in the wrong role is usually easiest to fix; ego the hardest.
3. **Is someone generating current?** Who creates energy daily, especially on bad days? Which levers are used — excitement, incentives, inspiration, speed/deadlines (public commitments strongest), fear? Recommend fewer fear levers, more speed + incentives. Check whether the leader has a purpose that prevents burnout.
4. **Incentive design.** What does each person want — money, status within their peer world, purpose, mastery? Test **story fit**: does the incentive fit how the person sees themselves and wants to be seen? More money can lose to the right status or freedom. Make mastery visible: current level, next level, the gap.
5. **Write it down.** A one-page plan: the goal, what good looks like, each person's exact task, incentive, deadline, and known blockers. The team sees a fraction of the leader's picture; the document must make the whole picture visible.
6. **Closed-system audit (four pillars, score 0–2 each):** shared direction/stories (written); celebration/rituals at milestones; rules with real enforcement; external validation (paying customers, credible outsiders). Fix the weakest first.
7. **Accept imperfect coordination.** Don't chase 100% alignment. Secure a coordinated core focused on growth and resolve conflicts among leaders quickly.

## Output
Diagnosis (one paragraph), coordination-cost table, top 3 blockages to remove this week, incentive changes, closed-system scores, and a draft of the written plan.

## Questions to ask
- "What is stopping each person from doing their best work today?"
- "If I asked your team what they're aiming for this year, would they give the same answer?"
- "Who on the team would you not rehire — and what is that costing everyone else?"
