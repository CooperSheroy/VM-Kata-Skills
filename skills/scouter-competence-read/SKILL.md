# Scouter Competence Read

## Source
Varun Mayya, *Why Indians Don't Attempt (And Stay Average Forever) | KATA 4* (YouTube, January 2026): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mPjj7-eHw1s
A distilled, paraphrased method — not a substitute for watching the talk. The talk's India framing is one example of a culture that discourages attempts; the method applies anywhere.

## Overview
Competence is read from **attempts made with personal risk**, not from opinions, predictions, credentials, or niceness. A clean, visible attempt raises standing even when it fails; one proven win upgrades it permanently.

## When to use
- **Partners and leaders:** sizing up a co-founder, boss, investor, mentor, or partner by their track record of attempts.
- **Self / career:** "why am I not trusted yet?", stuck in planning or commentary mode, wanting more pay or responsibility.
- **Negotiation:** deciding what an unproven person can reasonably ask for.
- **Culture:** keeping the bar high and rewarding doers over commentators.

## When not to use
- You are running a **hiring decision or process** (scorecard, interview design, retention, job-seeker coaching) → use **champr-hiring-lens**. Tie-break: scouter = *what level of credibility has this person earned through attempts?* (any relationship, including yourself); CHAMPR = *should we hire/keep this person for this role?* For a hire, run CHAMPR and use the scouter ladder as evidence for Potential and Reassurance.
- The person has a goal and is losing conviction after rejections → use **belief-to-behavior**.
- The person is torn between options → use **decide-and-commit**.

## Steps
**The ladder (score evidence, not claims)**

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| 0 Consumer | No stated beliefs, no attempts |
| 1 Commentator | Strong opinions, no attempts, no P&L |
| 2 Local doer | Self-initiated small attempts, low risk, some wins |
| 3 Owner | Repeated high-risk attempts with skin in the game; history of failures and at least one win |
| 4 Kingmaker | Multiple wins; can move a market/industry/culture; failures barely dent credibility |

**Reading someone else**
1. List their last 3–5 *attempts*: what they tried, what was at personal risk, outcome.
2. For failures: do they explain them without blaming external factors (including "I chose that company/partner")?
3. Do they update their model after mistakes, or repeat them?
4. Behaviour signals: do they kill bad ideas, say no, ask uncomfortable questions, resolve (not avoid) conflict, sell?
5. Discount: quietness mistaken for depth, credentials, eloquent critiques of others' work.
6. Place them on the ladder; note confidence and what evidence would move them.

**Raising your own level**
1. Name one belief you hold that others are skeptical about.
2. Design a clean, visible attempt that tests it, with real personal stake (time, money, reputation, a pay trade-off).
3. If it feels impossible, shrink the problem until it's finishable — don't swap in excuses.
4. Expect 5–10 attempts per success; plan the sequence.
5. Struggle visibly; ask for help; collect actionable feedback, ignore ridicule.
6. After each attempt, write what you learned and what you'll change.

**Applications**
- *Hiring signal:* prefer proof of work under risk over CVs; ask "tell me about something you tried that could have publicly failed."
- *Choosing a boss/co-founder:* avoid people who've never taken personal risk; they won't understand the trade-offs.
- *Negotiation:* proven outcomes justify asks; unproven beliefs start with skepticism — offer a risk-shared deal to prove it.
- *Team culture:* never lower the bar; strong people attract strong people.

## Output
Ladder placement with the attempt evidence, confidence level, what evidence would move them up, and (for self-use) the next clean attempt with its stake and deadline.

## Red flags
- Lots of predictions, zero swings.
- Wants full market salary plus founder-level upside with no risk.
- Silent for weeks on a hard task.
- Repeats the same failure pattern.

## Questions to ask
- "What have you attempted that others doubted?"
- "What did you personally risk?"
- "What went wrong, and what was your part in it?"
- "What's the smallest version you could finish this month?"
